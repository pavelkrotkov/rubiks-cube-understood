Lesson 5 · Middle Layer

The Middle Layer

Placing edges into a layer with solved layers on both sides — using a mirror-image pair, and understanding why it's a pair.

Your first layer is done (Lesson 4). Keep white down, yellow up. Now we fill the four edges of the middle layer — the trickiest-seeming step, because the slot you're filling has a solved layer right below it. The magic is an algorithm that opens a slot, tucks an edge in, and puts everything back.

Which pieces even go here?

The last layer will be yellow, so any edge with yellow on it belongs on top, not here. That means the four middle edges are exactly the ones with no yellow. So the first move is always: find a top-layer edge that has no yellow.

Why "no yellow" is the whole filter Every edge has two colors. A middle-layer edge sits between two side centers (say blue and red) — neither of which is yellow. So if a top edge shows yellow, it can't live in the middle; it's a last-layer piece. This one rule tells you which edges to grab and which to ignore.

Step 1 — align, to see where it goes

Take a no-yellow top edge. Turn the top (U) until its front-facing color matches the center of that front face — the edge and center make a little upside-down T. Now the edge's other color (the one pointing up) tells you its destination:

  • Up-color matches the right center → it goes right.
  • Up-color matches the left center → it goes left.

Front view. The small square above the face is the edge's up-facing sticker, folded toward you. Solid bottom row = your finished first layer; beige = unsolved middle edges.

Step 2 — send it with the matching algorithm

Right insertion

U R U' R' U' F' U F

Left insertion (the exact mirror)

U' L' U L U F U' F'

Both live on your algorithm cheat-sheet.

Why they're mirror images — the real insight Look closely: the left algorithm is the right algorithm reflected — every R becomes an L, and every turn's direction reverses: U↔U', F↔F', R↔L'. Check it move by move — the two sequences pair up perfectly under that rule. That's not a coincidence you must memorize twice; it's one idea seen in a mirror. The cube is symmetric left-to-right, so the move that tucks an edge rightward, reflected, tucks an edge leftward. Learn one and you half-know the other. This reflection symmetry is a genuine structural property of the cube group — the same operation conjugated by a mirror.
Why the layer below survives The algorithm briefly lifts a first-layer corner out of the way, slides the edge into the middle slot, and then reverses the disturbance — the later moves undo the earlier ones, restoring the white layer. Same surgical principle as the corner trigger: disturb, insert, restore.
Do this now Find a no-yellow top edge, align it to its front center (upside-down T), read its up-color, and run the matching algorithm slowly. Watch a corner briefly rise and settle back as the edge drops into the middle. Place all four.

The stuck-edge case

Sometimes every no-yellow edge is already down in the middle layer but in the wrong slot or flipped. There's nothing in the top to work with. Fix: run either algorithm to insert any top edge into that slot — this kicks the wrong edge up into the top layer. Now it's available; align it and insert it properly.

How to verify

Two full layers should now be solid: the entire white face, and the top two-thirds of every side face forming matching blocks. Only the yellow (top) layer remains scrambled.

Stuck or curious? If you can't form the upside-down T, you're probably trying to match the edge's up-facing color instead of its front-facing one — turn U and watch the front sticker only. If an edge sits in its correct middle slot but flipped, treat it as stuck: kick it out by inserting any top edge into that slot, then place it properly.
