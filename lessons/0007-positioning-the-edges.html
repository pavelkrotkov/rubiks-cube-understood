Lesson 7 · Last Layer

Positioning the Cross Edges

Orientation is done; now the other half of the split — permutation. Same pieces, different question.

You have a yellow cross (Lesson 6): all four edges are correctly oriented — yellow up. But look at their side stickers: they probably don't match the side centers. The pieces are facing the right way in the wrong homes. In Lesson 1 language: orientation solved, permutation not. This lesson fixes exactly that, and nothing else.

Step 1 — align for free with U

Before any algorithm, turn just the top (U) and watch the four edge side-stickers sweep past the centers. Check all four stops and settle on the best one — the stop where the most edges match. At that best stop, one of three things happens:

  • All four match — this step is already done; skip ahead.
  • Two adjacent edges match (they share a corner of the cube).
  • Two opposite edges match (they face each other).

At the other, non-best stops you may well see one match or none — that's normal. The guarantees below are about the best stop, and about "three" anywhere.

Why the best stop always shows four or exactly two Each of the four edges matches its center at exactly one of the four U stops. So across the four stops, the match-counts always add up to exactly 4. That little fact does real work: no stop can ever show three matches (if three edges are home, the fourth has nowhere else to go — it must be home too, making four). And the counts can't be spread as 1+1+1+1: each edge's "stops needed to get home" is just a position difference around one shared ring, so the four of them always add up to a multiple of 4 — but four all-different offsets, 0+1+2+3, add to 6. Impossible. So the best stop is always 4, or a 2 (adjacent or opposite) sharing its budget with lesser stops.

Step 2 — the swap algorithm

R U R' U R U2 R' U

This swaps the front and left top edges with each other (and leaves the back and right ones in place). So:

  • Two adjacent match: hold the cube so the two matched edges are at the back and right — the two wrong ones are now at front and left. Apply the algorithm once; all four edges land home (the trailing U is the realignment).
  • Two opposite match: no grip puts both wrong ones at front-left, so apply the algorithm once from any angle. This scrambles the matching into the adjacent case — realign with U and finish as above.
Why the cross doesn't fall apart Look at what the algorithm is made of: only R and U moves (you may recognize the trigger's DNA in it). Every edge it touches gets shuttled between top-layer edge slots and comes back yellow-side-up. It's a pure permutation of the cross pieces — it rearranges where they sit without disturbing how they face. That's the orientation/permutation split working for you: this tool moves pieces without twisting them, just as Lesson 6's tool twisted pieces without (net) moving the layers below.
Do this now Solve to the yellow cross. Turn U slowly and count matches at each of the four stops — confirm the four counts add up to 4, that no stop shows exactly three, and that the best stop shows four or exactly two. Then hold and swap as instructed. Verify: all four cross edges match their side centers and yellow still faces up on each. Heads-up: the yellow corners will visibly scramble while the algorithm runs — that's expected and allowed; they're the next lesson's problem.

Where you now stand

Everything on the cube is solved except the four yellow corners. And they break down the same way the edges just did: first put them in the right homes (permutation, Lesson 8), then twist them the right way (orientation, Lesson 9 — the final step, and where you'll meet the commutator).

Stuck or curious? A worthwhile puzzle: can R-and-U-only algorithms ever break the bottom two layers? Don't assume — trace it. R visibly dips into the bottom layers (it carries a bottom corner and edge up its right column), so "R and U can't touch the bottom" is false; what saves you is that this algorithm returns every borrowed piece before it ends. That's a property of the sequence, not the letters. If your edges end up in a state the two cases don't seem to cover, re-find the best U stop — a mis-count there is the usual cause. When your cross edges all match, continue to Lesson 8: positioning the corners — the finish line is in sight.
