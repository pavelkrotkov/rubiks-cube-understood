Lesson 6 · Last Layer

The Yellow Cross

Orientation, isolated — and your first glimpse of conjugation: an algorithm you already know, wrapped in a setup.

Two layers are solved (Lesson 5). Flip the cube so yellow is now on top. The last layer is solved in stages, and here's the elegant part tied to everything you've learned: the last layer splits cleanly into orientation then position. This lesson does orientation for the edges only — we make the yellow cross and ignore where the pieces sit.

Why we deliberately ignore position now Remember from Lesson 1: a piece has a position and an orientation. The last-layer method attacks them one at a time. Right now we only care that each yellow edge has its yellow sticker facing up — not whether it's in the right spot. Splitting a hard problem into orientation-then-position is the core trick of the whole last layer.

The one algorithm

F R U R' U' F'

Look at just the yellow edges on top (ignore corners). You'll see one of three patterns. The procedure is a loop: look at the pattern, hold the cube correctly for it, apply the algorithm once, then stop and look again before the next application — the pattern (and the correct grip) changes each round:

  • Dot — no yellow edges up. Any grip works this round. Expect 3 rounds total.
  • L-shape — two yellow edges forming a bend. Hold so the bend sits at the back and left, then apply. Expect 2 rounds from here.
  • Line — two yellow edges in a row. Hold it horizontal (left–right), then apply. 1 round from here.

Top views of the yellow face, front toward you. Beige = any color may be there; pale = corners, ignored this step.

Why the re-look between rounds is not optional Each application walks the pattern forward — dot → L → line → cross — but the new pattern lands wherever the algorithm leaves it, not where you'd next need to hold it. From a dot, the L appears at the front-right, and you must bring it to back-left before the next application. Blindly applying three times without re-gripping walks you in a circle, right back to the dot. One algorithm, yes — but re-aim between shots.
Self-check: nothing changed? If you apply the algorithm and the edge pattern looks identical, you held an L with its bend at the back-right — the one grip where the algorithm's effect cancels out on the pattern. Rotate the top two yellow edges to the back-left and go again.
The big idea: this is conjugation Read the algorithm as brackets: F  (R U R' U')  F'. The middle is your corner trigger from Lesson 4. The outer F and F' are a setup and its undo: F moves top edges out of the top layer, into the trigger's reach, the trigger does its flipping work, and F' puts the front back. That pattern — do X, act, undo X — is called conjugation, and it's how cubers aim a known tool at a new target. You're not learning a random six-move string; you're seeing a tool you already own, pointed somewhere new. Conjugation is a central move in group theory: same operation, viewed from a shifted setup.
Do this now Get to the last layer, yellow up. Run the loop: look at the edge pattern, grip per the list above, apply F R U R' U' F' once, then stop and re-look before going again. Watch the pattern climb dot → L → line → cross round by round. Don't worry that colors on the sides don't match yet — that's the next lesson.

How to verify

The top face shows a solid yellow plus sign (center + four edges). The four yellow corners are probably still wrong — that's expected. Only the cross matters here.

Stuck or curious? The two failure modes here are both grip mistakes: the pattern didn't change (L held at back-right — see the self-check above), or the pattern went backwards toward the dot (line held vertical instead of horizontal, or an un-regripped L). Either way the fix is the same: stop, re-read the pattern, re-grip, apply once. You can't damage anything below — the algorithm only ever touches the last layer's arrangement — so mis-grips cost a round, never the solve. Once you have a solid yellow cross, continue to Lesson 7: positioning the last-layer edges.
