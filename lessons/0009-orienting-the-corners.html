Lesson 9 · Last Layer · Final Step

Orienting the Corners

The last step — with a moment where the cube looks destroyed and you keep going anyway, because the structure says so.

Every piece is home (Lesson 8). All that remains is the last unchecked box from Lesson 1: some corners are correctly positioned but wrongly twisted. Fix their orientation and the cube is solved. This step's algorithm is tiny — the skill is holding your nerve.

The algorithm

R' D' R D

Yes — it's the Lesson 4 trigger's cousin, aimed downward. And you already know what shape it is: A B A′ B′ with A = R', B = D' — a small commutator, so it changes little and undoes itself predictably.

The procedure — read all of it before starting

  1. Hold the cube yellow-up, with a twisted corner at front-right-top.
  2. Repeat R' D' R D until that corner's yellow faces up. It always takes 2 or 4 repetitions (the twist steps 120° per pair) — so check only after every second repetition. Mid-pair, a bottom-layer corner visits the slot and may even show white facing up; ignore whatever colors pass through between pairs.
  3. Without rotating the cube in your hands, turn only the top (U) to bring the next twisted corner into the front-right-top spot. Never turn D between corners — the bottom must stay exactly as the loan left it. Repeat step 2.
  4. When every corner is yellow-up, a final turn of U (one, two, or three quarter-turns) aligns the top layer. The cube is solved.
The nerve-holding part — why the "damage" is a loan Midway through, the bottom two layers will look wrecked. Every beginner panics here and "fixes" it — destroying their solve. Don't. Each round of R' D' R D borrows from the bottom layers and doesn't fully repay until the corner twists land back at a clean multiple. Because the total twist of all corners must cancel out (see the law below), the number of repetitions across the whole step always comes out to a full cycle — and the bottom layers snap back exactly as the last corner finishes. The mess was never damage; it was debt with guaranteed repayment. Trust the structure — you've seen why commutators and repetition cycles restore what they disturb. Prove the load-bearing fact yourself: on a solved cube, apply R' D' R D six times — it returns exactly. That order-6 cycle is what the repayment rides on.
The twist law — your first impossibility proof Here's the payoff fact: the total twist of the eight corners is conserved — every face turn changes it by a multiple of 3 (i.e., not at all, mod 3). Consequence: a cube with one single corner twisted and everything else solved is unsolvable by turning faces. If you ever meet one, someone twisted a corner with their thumb — pop it back physically. This is why your repetition counts in step 2 always conspire to a clean total: the cube literally cannot leave you with leftover twist. This conservation law is worked out properly in Janet Chen's notes — the same reasoning shows a lone flipped edge is impossible too.
Do this now — finish your first cube Solve through Lesson 8, then run this step. Say it aloud at the scary moment: "the bottom is fine, it's a loan." When the last corner clicks yellow and the layers snap back, give the final U. Look at what's in your hands. You solved it — and you know why every step worked.
