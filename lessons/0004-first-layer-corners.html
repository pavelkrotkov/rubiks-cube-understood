Lesson 4 · First Layer

First-Layer Corners

Your first real algorithm — and we dissect exactly why it works, so it's understanding, not incantation.

You have a verified white cross on the bottom (Lesson 3). Keep white down, yellow up. Now we fill the four bottom corners to finish the entire first layer. This is the first time you'll memorize a move sequence — but you won't memorize it blind. You'll watch it do its job.

The goal

Each corner has three colors. One of the four white corners has, say, white-blue-red — its one true home is where the white, blue, and red faces meet. When all four white corners sit correctly, the whole bottom face is solid white and the bottom band of every side face matches its center. First layer complete.

The setup: get the corner above its slot

Work one corner at a time. The target slot is the bottom-front-right (where the front and right faces meet, on the bottom). Always insert there, rotating the whole cube to bring each new slot to the front-right.

  1. Find a white corner that belongs in the front-right-bottom slot (its colors match the front center, the right center, and white).
  2. If it's loose in the top layer, turn U until it sits at the top-front-right position — directly above its slot. "Above its slot" = its two non-white colors are the front and right center colors — in either arrangement. When white faces straight up, the two side colors look swapped relative to their centers; that still counts as above its slot.
  3. If it's stuck in a bottom slot but wrong, do R U R' once to pop it up into the top layer, then position it as above.

The algorithm: the trigger, repeated

With the corner at top-front-right, repeat this single sequence until the corner drops into place with white facing down:

R U R' U' — at most 5 times

That's it. One trigger. The corner enters the slot on the odd repetitions and pops back up on the even ones, so check it whenever it's down: it seats correctly — white facing the floor, side colors matching — after exactly 1, 3, or 5 repetitions, depending on which way white was facing at the start. If it's rep 4 and things look wrong, you're not failing; the 5th finishes it.

Why this works — watch it, don't just trust it The repetitions alternate: an odd rep drops the corner into the slot (possibly twisted), the following even rep lifts it back out — and each round trip re-enters the slot one twist further along. The corner can sit in the slot three ways, but only one has white facing down, so the cycle must hit it within three visits (reps 1, 3, 5). You're not hoping; you're walking a piece through a known, finite set of states. Formally: the trigger has finite order — repeat it enough and the cube returns to start (6 times here). You're using a slice of that cycle to land the orientation you want.
Why the cross survives Notice R U R' U' only ever disturbs the right column and the top — and the R' puts back whatever R moved. So the white cross you worked for stays intact through every repetition. Good algorithms are surgical: they change a few pieces and leave the rest alone.
Do this now — narrate it Place one white corner above its slot. Do R U R' U' once and watch the corner: see it dip into the slot and pop back twisted. Keep going, out loud — "still up… twisted… down, white facing the wrong way… there." Feel the cycle. Then solve all four corners this way.

How to verify

Turn the cube so white is up and look: the entire white face is solid white, and each side face shows a matching solid band across the first layer. If a corner shows white on a side instead of down, it's seated in the wrong orientation — pop it out with R U R' and redo the trigger.

Before inserting, where must the white corner sit?
Why does repeating R U R' U' eventually seat the corner correctly?
Why is the white cross undisturbed by R U R' U'?

Stuck or curious? If the trigger seems to loop forever, stop and re-check the staging: the corner must be at top-front-right, and its two non-white colors must be the front and right center colors (in either arrangement — re-read the sidenote if white faces up). After 5 reps it must be seated; if it isn't, it was staged over the wrong slot — turn U and re-aim. Faster three-case versions of this step exist (see the Ruwix link above) — learn them after the trigger feels boring, not before. Once your whole first layer is solid and verified, continue to Lesson 5: the middle layer.
