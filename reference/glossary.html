Rubik's Cube · Reference

Glossary & Notation

The shared vocabulary for this course. Every lesson uses these terms exactly.

Notation — the six face turns

Hold the cube still and name faces by where they are relative to you. A bare letter = quarter turn (90°) clockwise as you look at that face.

LetterFaceTurn
UUp (top)top face 90° clockwise
DDown (bottom)bottom face 90° clockwise
LLeftleft face 90° clockwise
RRightright face 90° clockwise
FFront (facing you)front face 90° clockwise
BBackback face 90° clockwise

Modifiers

SymbolMeansExample
' (prime)counter-clockwise quarter turnR' = right face anticlockwise
2half turn (180°), direction doesn't matterU2 = top face twice

"Clockwise" always means as if you were looking straight at that face — so L and R turn in opposite real-world directions.

The pieces

Cubie / piece
One of the small cubes. There are 26, but the cube behaves as 20 movable pieces plus 6 fixed centers.
Center
The single sticker in the middle of each face. Centers never move relative to each other — they define each face's color. 6 of them.
Edge
A piece with two stickers, sitting between two centers. 12 of them.
Corner
A piece with three stickers. 8 of them.
Facelet / sticker
A single colored tile. Useful to point at, but not the right unit of thinking — pieces are.

The two things you can do to a piece

Permutation
Which position a piece is in — is it in the right spot? (Where it lives.)
Orientation
How a piece is twisted/flipped within its spot — is it facing the right way? (How it sits.)
Solved
Every piece is both correctly permuted and correctly oriented.

Method & algorithm terms

Algorithm (alg)
A memorized sequence of moves that produces a known, predictable change to a few pieces.
Inverse
The undo of a move or algorithm — same moves backwards, each direction reversed. The inverse of R U is U' R'.
Layer
A 3×3 slice of the cube. The beginner method solves the cube one layer at a time: first, middle, last.
Layer-by-layer (LBL)
The beginner method this course follows. Singmaster popularized it in 1979.
Terms drift in the wild. Other guides say "permute/orient the last layer" (PLL/OLL) for what Lessons 6–9 do, and "F2L" for the first two layers. The concepts are the ones defined here. If a term feels fuzzy, revisit Lesson 1 and Lesson 2, where this vocabulary is built hands-on.
