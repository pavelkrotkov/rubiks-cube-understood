A course in solving the 3×3

The Rubik's Cube, Understood

Solve any scramble — and know why every move works.

Most guides hand you sequences to memorize. This course takes the other road: it builds the cube's logic first — pieces, position, orientation — and then introduces each algorithm as something with a visible reason: a repetition cycle, a mirror image, a setup-and-undo (conjugation), a do-undo interleaving (commutator), a conservation law. By the end you can solve the cube from memory and explain, stage by stage, why the method works — including why some cube states are flat-out impossible.

How to use this course Work with a real 3×3 in hand — every lesson has "do this now" steps that only pay off physically. Take the lessons in order; each is short and ends with self-checking quizzes and a verification step. Don't move on until the verification passes on your own cube. Two reference pages — the glossary and the algorithm cheat-sheet — are the pages you'll come back to after the lessons fade.

  • 08Positioning the Corners — a three-piece cycle that is secretly a commutator — the deepest trick in cubing.
  • 09Orienting the Corners — the finish: a four-move loan with guaranteed repayment, and your first impossibility proof.

Sources this course stands on

The mathematical spine — permutation vs. orientation, conjugates, commutators, the conservation laws — follows Janet Chen, Group Theory and the Rubik's Cube and Tom Davis, Group Theory via Rubik's Cube. The move sequences are the standard beginner layer-by-layer method as documented by Ruwix, a lineage going back to Singmaster's 1979 notes.

When you outgrow this course

Post your first solve — or your best "why does this work?" question — at r/Cubers or the Speedsolving forums. From there the usual roads are speed (the CFOP method), bigger cubes, or the real group theory in Chen's notes — for which this course has quietly prepared you.
